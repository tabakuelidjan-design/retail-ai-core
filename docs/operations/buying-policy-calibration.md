# Buying policy calibration worksheet

Every merchant policy value below is a **decision**, not a default. While a value is unset (`null`) the dependent check is `INCOMPLETE` and the verdict is `NEED MORE DATA` — the system never invents a number and never compares a `null` as zero. Use `npm run buying:calibrate -- <candidate.json> --policy <baseline.json>` to see how a candidate's verdict moves when one setting changes at a time. Its probe values are a grid around *your* baseline; they are not recommendations.

Policy file: `data/local/buying-policy.json` (gitignored). Template: `docs/examples/buying-policy.example.json`.

| Setting | Controls | Too strict | Too loose | Data that helps you choose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `testBudget` | The most capital one standard test may put at risk. Compared with landed cost × quantity; a quoted minimum order above it, even at the most favourable cost, is a robust `AVOID`. | Good products are rejected only because the minimum order is large; you never learn. | A wrong test costs real cash you cannot afford to lose; many tests add up. | Cash you can lose without harm; weekly net sales; how many tests you want running at once. |
| `exploratoryBudget` | A stricter cap for exploratory tests (no usable internal peers). Only used when exploratory tests are on. | You never test genuinely new categories. | "Exploratory" becomes an excuse for large bets with no evidence. | Keep it a small fraction of the standard budget; it is the price of learning about an unknown category. |
| `allowExploratoryTests` | Whether a candidate without a usable peer benchmark can still become an `EXPLORATORY` test. Peer-demand checks are waived; margin, capability, lead time and capital still apply. | (off) New categories always end in `NEED MORE DATA`. | (on) Untested ideas reach the buying table. | Whether the merchant wants to explore categories with no internal history. |
| `minUnitMarginPct` | The unit margin (ex tax, after payment cost) a candidate must clear. Only a decided price and firm costs can reject on it. | Reasonable items fail; the hurdle may exceed what the category can earn. | Thin-margin items pass, then cost overheads not modelled here (production, packaging, labour). | Current unit margins **once costs are verified**; whether unit cost is all-in (see `gates.unitCostIsAllInVariableCost`). Until then treat existing margins as upper bounds. |
| `paymentCostPct` | Processor cost as a share of retail ex tax, deducted from margin. `0` is a valid explicit value. | Margin is understated. | Margin is overstated. | The real payment mix: share by gateway and the fees actually reported (see the payment-fee triage). |
| `taxRateAssumption` | Converts a tax-inclusive expected price to ex tax when the candidate doesn't state a rate. It is an assumption and keeps the margin conditional. | — | A wrong rate silently shifts every margin. | The confirmed tax treatment of the product class; or enter prices ex tax and leave it unset. |
| `maxSellThroughWeeks` | How long a test quantity may take to sell at the median selling peer's pace. A failure asks for a smaller quantity or more evidence; it never rejects alone. | Almost every MOQ looks too big. | Slow-moving quantities look fine and tie up stock. | Peer velocities by category (`buying:peer-sets`), how long stock may sit before it hurts cash. |
| `maxLeadTimeDays` | Longest acceptable supplier lead time; a *quoted* longer time is a robust `AVOID`. Unset = lead time is not checked (stated in the result). | Slow suppliers are rejected outright. | Late arrival for the selling window it was bought for. | The date the goods must be on sale by; the tool prints the arrival date if ordered now. |
| `exposure.noSaleShare` | Share of a peer set's stock with no sale in the window at or above which it is "saturated". | Healthy categories look saturated. | Real over-stock is not noticed. | The distribution of no-sale share across your peer sets. |
| `exposure.coverWeeks` | Weeks of cover at or above which peer stock counts as saturated. | As above. | As above. | Peer cover weeks; keep in mind history is short. |
| `stockTrust.blockedShare` | Share of a peer set's units that are suspect / stale / uncounted-suspect at which exposure is `BLOCKED` (no verdict on stock). | Exposure is blocked almost everywhere; nothing can be judged. | Exposure is judged on numbers that may be placeholders. | How much peer stock is flagged suspect (`peer-sets` output). |
| `stockTrust.trustedShare` | Share of a peer set's units that must be physically counted (and still reconcile) before its stock is `TRUSTED` and can support an `AVOID`. | You must count almost everything before any rejection. | Rejections rest on a small counted fraction. | How many variants make up most of the peer stock (`--count-sheet` lists them by units). |
| `stockTrust.unverifiedMaySupportPass` | May never-counted quantities support a **pass** on exposure? (They can never support a rejection.) `null` = strict. | Every candidate waits for stock counts. | A pass can rest on stock that is really higher than Shopify shows. | Your confidence in Shopify quantities for that category. |
| `gates.unitCostIsAllInVariableCost` | Set `true` only when the unit cost already includes every variable cost. Until then margin-based conclusions on *existing* products stay gated. | — | Margins that omit production/packaging look real. | Whether a production-cost module or configuration exists for the category. |
| `estimateTolerancePct` (core default 0.25) | How far an ESTIMATED input may be off when testing whether a conclusion could flip. | A rejection needs almost certainty. | Estimates are trusted too much: more rejections on soft numbers. | How good your past supplier estimates have been vs final quotes. |

## Reading a sensitivity run

- Settings that never flip a verdict for your typical candidates do not need fine tuning.
- A setting whose neighbouring values flip `TEST CANDIDATE` ↔ `NEED MORE DATA` ↔ `AVOID FOR NOW` is where your judgement matters most: decide it deliberately.
- If `AVOID FOR NOW` appears only when a threshold is tightened past a *quoted* fact (e.g. lead time), that is a policy choice, not a data problem.
